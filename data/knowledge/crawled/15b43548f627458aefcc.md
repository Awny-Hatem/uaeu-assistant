The UAEU and You

The purpose of UAEU is to advance knowledge, serve society,
and to develop future leaders.  This means that at UAEU,
you will develop specialist and general knowledge, you
will contribute to the university community, and you will
build and demonstrate leadership.  Your achievements and
success are the value of the university.
A university is a unique organization. It expects you to take
responsibility, to respect others, to behave honestly, and to
demonstrate integrity.  You must uphold the highest ethical
standards in your personal conduct, academic work, and
dealings with others in the university community.
This is a demanding agenda. You have a challenging few
years ahead. University is not an easy time or place.  However,
we can make some promises to you.

        ACADEMIC HONESTY

        In order to learn you will read and listen to other people’s work.  You will also
        write about what you have learnt f rom reading and listening to the work of
        others.  You must make it clear when the ideas you are describing are yours and
        when they belong to other people. If you use the work of other people but do
        not acknowledge them, you are not honest in academic terms.  The technical
        term for lack of academic honesty is ‘plagiarism’.  You need to learn how to
        avoid plagiarism. There are many resources online (search avoid plagiarism).
        YOU MUST NOT:

        •                                                          Try to get credit for work that is not your own.
        •                                      Copy text and submit it as your own (give the impression it is your own
               because you have not said who wrote it first … )
        •                                                          Copy text, change a few words, and submit it as your own
        •                                                                    Take text f   rom different places, piece it together, and submit it as your own.
        •                                       Download text, pictures, charts, data f rom the internet, and submit it as
               your own.
        •                                                          Quote                      what Person 2 has written about what Person 1 has written, but only
               acknowledge Person 1.
        YOU MUST:

        •                                           Identify the author and source of any text you have copied, paraphrased,
               pieced together, or downloaded.

        •                                     Identify the author and source of any ideas, research, theories, concepts
               you are using.
        IF IN DOUBT:

        •                                                          Ask

        •                                                          Acknowledge the original author in your writing.

        •                                                               List all the texts or sources you have used at the end of your paper.  This is a
               ‘bibliography’ or ‘ reference list’.

        What happens if you commit plagiarism?  Level 1 Plagiarism: Before you have
        completed 24 credit hours or if the plagiarism is small, we will give you extra
        training to make sure you understand citation and referencing.  We might also
        reduce your grades or ask you to do the work again. Level 2 Plagiarism:  After 24
        credit hours, or when the plagiarism is large we can fail the work, fail the course,
        suspend you f  rom the university or dismiss you.

       STUDENT CLUBS AND SOCIETIES

       The university has many clubs and societies that you can join.  You can find
       out about more in the Student Life Department                             .  You can also set up a club or
       society – you must get approval and follow the regulations in the Student Life
       Policy Manual.

       Student clubs and societies give you the opportunity to develop your own
       interests. Some might extend what you learn in class, some might be about
       other topics.  Clubs and societies give you the opportunity to develop skills you
       need after you graduate, and to contribute to the community.  The students
       who run clubs and are volunteers. Clubs and societies have a clear purpose and
       leadership structure that includes staff/faculty advisers.  The advisers guide the
       students on their activities and the university regulations.  Students who want
       to set up a club or society must have a GPA of more than 2.0 and must not have
       disciplinary sanctions. Only approved clubs and societies can apply for financial
       support, equipment and use of university facilities.

DISCIPLINE
You are expected to keep to the Student Code of Conduct (Student Life Deanship
Policy Manual - Policy and Procedures 2). If your behavior does not meet these
standards the UAEU will take you through a discipline process.  You can see the
detail of these in the Procedures 2 of the Student Life Deanship Policy Manual at
sections 3 to 13.

The goal of the discipline process is to encourage you to keep the standards of
behavior we expect.  The sanctions and penalties are incentives and support for
good conduct.  Repeat misconduct and severe misconduct results in the sanctions
and penalties becoming harsh – more like punishment.  The community is also
expected to keep a check on itself.  This means that your ‘bad’ behavior can be
called out by anyone.

Important to know:

•                                                          No one making an allegation will suffer
•                                                          An accused student is innocent until the allegation is proven
•                        A discipline committee makes the investigation; it is impartial and uses
      evidence.
•                                                       Non-academic misconduct: the Associate Provost for Student Affairs leads the
      process
•                                                          Academic misconduct: the Dean of College leads the process
•                                                                Appeals by the student: the Provost considers if the process has been fair and if
      the decision/sanction is reasonable.
•                                                           The Vice Chancellor has to approve the most serious punishments of dismissal
      or withdrawal of the degree certificate/qualification.

         PRIVACY AND CONFIDENTIALITY
         The university has strict rules about the information it keeps on you and how it
         can be used (academic records)

         •                                                        We will keep copies of your photo, passport, Emirates ID, Family Book, and
               CV.
         •                                                    We will hold a record of your mobile number, personal email address, and
               emergency contact.
         •                                            We will keep a file of your admission form, immigration form, and health
               insurance form.
         •                                                          Your academic records are the property of the University Registrar.
         •                                                          Your disciplinary record is held by the University Registrar.

         The Dean of Enrollment and University Registrar manages a protocol that sets
         out who can get access to your information.  Access rights depend on whether
         a person needs to know that information in order to do their job.  For example,
         a faculty member or academic adviser will need to see your academic records,
         but not your insurance details. No one who gets access to your information is
         allowed to give that information to another person without authorization by
         you or the Dean of Enrollment and University Registrar.

         You can ask to see the information we hold about you.
         SAFETY
         Your psychological and physical safety is our priority.  This is why we have rules
         about your conduct on campus and your behavior to others. See the Students’
         Life Deanship Policy Manual (Procedures 2) (Student Code of Conduct) for
         detail of the behaviors that are risky or damaging to you and to others.
         The university has obtained OHSAS 18001 Certification. This confirms our ability
         to control security and contain risks.  So, for example we have health and safety
         rules that deal with

         •                                                          Urgent situations such as fire and emergency evacuations

         •                                                          First Aid

         •                                                          Use and behavior in laboratories

         •                                                          Dealing with hazardous substances and waste.

         You will receive training about these health and safety rules through seminars
         and a required course on occupational health and safety.
