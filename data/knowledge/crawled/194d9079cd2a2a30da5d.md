## Theme 2: Urban Waste Material Recycling
The objective of Theme 2 is to develop multi- stage valorization paths that efficiently recycle food and plant waste into the food supply chain and generate opportunities for start-ups and investment.

Modern food production and supply chains are often based on through-flow rather than recirculation of mineral nutritional elements, energy, and water. Resulting scarcity of natural resources and environmental pollution may become major threats to global food security unless efficient ways for recycling of organic waste materials into agricultural food production are developed and implemented. Abu Dhabi is producing around 1.3 Mio t of agricultural waste, and around 500.000 t of food waste annually. Recycling of organic waste materials is currently largely based on composting, which can return nutritional elements to agricultural soils, but leaves the energy and nutritional value organic residues contain unused. The development of more efficient valorization strategies for organic waste materials would not only save resources but also generate business opportunities as a circular economy evolves. Multi-level valorization strategies involving a sequence of different consecutive recycling procedures are still in their infancy in most parts of the world. The implementation of subthemes outlined in the table below would provide Abu Dhabi with the technologies and know-how to pioneer in this field. ​

## Subthemes​​
Insect farming on food and plant waste ​

Compared with conventional farm animals, insects require less space, energy and water to convert waste materials into animal protein of high quality. The ARIFSID established and operates the first lab for insect farming research and education in the UAE. The research focus lies on optimizing the production of black soldier flies and buffalo worms under climatic conditions of the UAE, test different feed sources and small-scale integrative production systems.

Working Groups:

​ Insect farm

Small-scale urban mushroom production ​

Edible mushrooms have a high nutritional and economic value. Most of them can be cultivated at various scales on organic waste materials. The ARIFSID runs a mushroom lab and production facility on the Al Foah Experimental farm and ​ strives to establish protocols by which mushrooms of high quality can be grown at a small scale in urban environments, giving rise to small-scale enterprises. The subtheme also seeks to establish methods for the cultivation of the precious desert truffles. These are ectomycorrhizal fungi living in symbioses with shrubs that could be grown in urban landscapes and gardens.

Working Groups: ​

Mushrooms

Nutrient solutions, growth substrates and organic fertilizers

The majority of biomass produced by the agricultural sector ends up as waste, e.g. in form of crop residues, food and kitchen waste. Unless properly recycled, this material becomes a main source or greenhouse gas emissions and ecosystem eutrophication. The ARIFSID develops technologies by which organic waste can be recycled into growth media and nutrient solutions for hydroponic plants, as well as media for plant materials grown in bioreactors.

Working Groups:

​ Growth substrates ​​

Use of functional food components from plant waste ​

Some organic waste can be a source of metabolites of medicinal, nutraceutical, or industrial value. This subtheme strives to identify, extract, and valorize these. It develops nutraceuticals, fibers, novel alternative protein, and materials for food packaging applications from date palm waste.

Working Groups:

​ Functional food ​
