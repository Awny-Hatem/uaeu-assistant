         4.1 Employment Information
                   This annual report used data gathered from two sources  by the Office of Institutional Effectiveness
              (OIE)  to  determine  the  percentage  of  completers  who  got  employed  within  nine  months  after
              graduation, and  b)      2023-2024 College Alumni and Employer’s Survey,                 administered by the CEDU
              Alumni Affairs Unit under the Office Assistant Dean of Student Affairs to  requests information about
              the  completers’  current  employment  status  and  information  (position,  company,  manager’s
              information, and reasons for unemployment).

              4.1.1    Graduate Destination Survey (GDS)
                            Table 4.1 displays the employment outcomes for graduates of Early Childhood Education
                       (ECE), Special Education (SPED), and Health and Physical Education (HPE) across multiple
                       cohorts. The data shown represent the most current figures, including previously reported
                       information,  and  are  drawn  from  the  University's  Academic  Analytics  dashboard,  the
                       designated source for reporting essential program performance metrics. The employment rate
                       is  determined  as  the  proportion  of  graduates  employed  within  nine  months  following
                       graduation, calculated by the formula: (Number Employed + Number Studying) divided by
                       (Total Responses minus the Number of Students Not Seeking Employment).

                            Across the three cohorts, ECE program            demonstrates steady improvement in graduate
                       employment  outcomes,  alongside  consistently  strong  response  rates.  The  response  rate
                       increased from 71.6% in 2021–2022 to 78.7% in 2023–2024, reflecting increasingly reliable
                       graduate follow‑up. Employment rates showed a notable upward trend, rising from 46.8% in
                       2021–2022 (N=48) and 48.8% in 2022–2023 (N= 42) to a substantially higher 75.0% in 2023–
                       2024    (N=37).  This  improvement  is  driven  by  an  increase  in  the  number  of  employed
                       respondents  and  a  corresponding  reduction  in  unemployment.  Across  all  cohorts,  no
                       graduates reported pursuing further study, indicating that employment outcomes serve as the
                       primary indicator of post‑graduation success for this program.

                            The SPED program shows a progressive increase in both engagement and employment
                       outcomes over time. Response rates improved markedly, from 63.2% in 2021–2022 (N=24) to
                       86.8%   in   2023–2024        (N=24),   strengthening   confidence   in   cohort   representativeness.
                       Employment rates increased from 45.8% (N=24) to 66.7% (N=33) across the three cohorts,

4.2 EPP Programs Supporting Completer Employment

                   The EPP maintains its partnership and collaboration with the MOE and ADEK schools, and other
              stakeholders  to  ensure  that  its  completers  are  provided  with  additional  qualifications  and
              opportunities for employment.

                   Below are some of the support programs provided to the completers:

                   1.   Teacher Licensing Certification. The EPP leverages the Ministry of Education's comprehensive
                        professional development workshops for internship students to strengthen their career skills
                        and   broaden   their   understanding   of   the   educational   sector.   To   gauge   candidates’
                        preparedness  for  licensure  examinations,  a  mock  test  is  conducted,  and  its  results  are
                        communicated to the EPP. Based on these results, the EPP designs targeted workshop sessions
                        to  equip  candidates  with  the  necessary  competencies.  This  systematic  process  ensures
                        students  are  thoroughly  prepared  for  licensure  assessments  and  their  future  roles  as
                        educators. Furthermore, the EPP consulted with the MOE to receive updates on the licensing
                        certification framework and teacher standards in the UAE, ensuring that program course
                        content and learning outcomes are realigned to the most relevant Elements, Performance
                        Standards, Criteria, and Indicators.

                   2.   Employment of Completers. The EPP maintains ongoing communication with the Ministry of
                        Education (MOE) to remain informed of current workforce needs and updates to professional
                        teaching  standards,  ensuring  that  program  completers  are  prepared  with  competencies
                        aligned with the UAE Vision and recognized international best practices. In addition, the EPP
                        actively  collaborates  with  partner  schools  (e.g.,  Liwa  School)  by  disseminating  teaching
                        vacancy announcements to completers to facilitate access to job opportunities. The Alumni
                        Office  further  supports  graduates  by  issuing  letters  of  recommendation,  coordinating
                        employment‑related documentation, and arranging job interviews with education employers
                        upon request. Collectively, these initiatives reflect the EPP’s proactive role in enhancing
                        graduate employability and supporting successful entry into the education workforce.

                   3.   Completer     Induction & Mentoring.          The EPP is committed to supporting newly employed
                        completers during their transition into the teaching profession. Feedback from employer focus
                        group discussions indicates that some completers experience initial challenges related to
                        workplace norms, feedback processes, and classroom management. In response, the EPP is
