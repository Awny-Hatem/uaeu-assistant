         2.1 Satisfaction of Employers

                   Employer satisfaction regarding the relevance and effectiveness of completers’ preparation for
              their professional responsibilities was measured through a survey administered by the Educator
              Preparation Provider (EPP) during the 2024-2025 academic year. Eighteen completers were evaluated
              by employers on their competencies across four domains outlined in the EPP's teaching framework:
              planning and preparation, classroom environment, instruction, and professional responsibility. The
              assessment employed the validated Employer Satisfaction Instrument, which includes 25 items rated
              on a scale from 0 to 4 (“Not prepared at all” to “Highly prepared”). Insights from this survey guide the
              EPP in identifying and implementing program enhancements to strengthen preparation of completers.

                   Employer survey results for the 2024-2025 academic year demonstrate consistently high levels of
              satisfaction and preparedness among completers, with overall averages concentrated completers as
              mostly well prepared to highly prepared across all assessed areas of preparation (see Table 1.2). In the
              area  of Planning  and  Preparation,  every  employer  rated  completers  as                    well  prepared  or  highly
              prepared, resulting in a mean score of 3.44. Similarly, the Classroom Environment domain received
              universal ratings of      well-prepared      or highly prepared, with an even higher mean of 3.54.                   For the
              Instruction domain, employers assessed completers as well or highly prepared (M=3.42). In the area of
              Professional  Responsibility,  all  employers          rated  completers       as  well-prepared      or  highly  prepared
              (M=3.37).

                   Overall satisfaction with the preparation of program completers was strong, as demonstrated by
              an aggregate mean score of 3.44 (N=18) based on responses from employers. These results indicate
              that EPP graduates are consistently viewed as highly prepared, with strength noted in the classroom
              environment domain.          Although responses were positive across all assessed areas, the                    EPP closely
              reviews subscale-level data to pinpoint opportunities for ongoing improvement, especially regarding
              professional responsibility. The EPP remains committed to leveraging these insights to further enhance
              the effectiveness of its programs and ensure graduates continue to meet professional standards.

                        Alongside the employer satisfaction survey, two focus group discussions were held in 2024-
                   2025 to gather meaningful perspectives on the strengths and areas needing improvement among
                   EPP program completers. The discussions revealed both the positive attributes of graduates and
                   the challenges they face when transitioning into professional roles.

                        Graduates entering the teaching profession are recognized by employers for their passion and
                   motivation, which serve as core strengths and lay the foundation for their long-term success.
                   However,  while  they  possess  strong  theoretical  knowledge,  many  lack  practical  classroom
                   experience, highlighting the need for more hands-on learning opportunities and mentorship.

                        Employers observed gaps in assessment and feedback skills, with completers often struggling
                   to   implement   effective   assessment   tools   and   provide   constructive   feedback.   Classroom
                   management and adaptability pose ongoing challenges, suggesting a need for targeted training in
                   these areas.

                        Graduates show openness to engage with diverse learners, including those with special needs,
                   but require further development in inclusive education practices. Professionalism and community
                   involvement are inconsistent, with some completers actively participating in school initiatives and
                   others needing greater preparation for responsibilities beyond teaching.

                        Finally, transitioning from university to workplace culture presents difficulties, especially in
                   areas like punctuality, communication, and school protocols. Employers recommend structured
                   orientation and workplace readiness programs to support this adjustment.

                        Overall, the EPP’s completers are recognized for their strong motivation and growth mindset,
                   which positively impacts their teaching effectiveness. However, there is a need to strengthen
                   practical classroom skills, assessment abilities, and adaptability through improved program design.

2.2 Involvement of internal and external stakeholders in program design, evaluation, and
                   continuous improvement processes

                   The    EPP   demonstrates   a   robust   commitment   to   continuous   program   improvement   by
              systematically integrating regular monitoring, evaluation, and progress tracking into all aspects of its
              operations. This commitment is reinforced through strategic partnerships with its partner schools and
              the Ministry of Education (MOE), which facilitate direct employment pathways for program completers
              and  ensure ongoing  alignment  with  professional standards.  Alumni  are  actively  engaged  in  EPP
              activities, including participation in professional forums             such as the Mydan Forum and dedicated
              alumni events, which provide valuable feedback and support program enhancement. Additionally, the
              EPP maintains a dynamic relationship with its graduates through ongoing communication by the
              Alumni Office and continuous updates to the alumni database, enabling effective tracking of graduate
              outcomes and informed decision-making for program development.

                   2.2.1 Partnerships and Collaborations

                               The EPP has an existing partnership and collaboration with partner schools and the MOE.
                         The partnership commits the EPP and its partners to the following:

                               Internship. The EPP actively fosters significant partnerships with both private and public
                         schools, providing valuable training and internship experiences for its candidates to enhance
                         their competitiveness for future employment. During Fall 2024, candidates from the ECE,
                         HPE, and SPED programs were placed at 22 partner schools and one government center,
                         while in Spring 2025, placements were made at 10 partner schools and one government
                         center. The program incorporates feedback from supervisors, cooperative teachers, and
                         candidates to continuously improve the internship experience.

                               Teacher  Licensing  Certification.         The  EPP  leverages  the  Ministry  of  Education's
                         comprehensive professional development workshops for internship students to strengthen
                         their career skills and broaden their understanding of the educational sector. To gauge
                         candidates’ preparedness for licensure examinations, a mock test is conducted, and its results
                         are communicated to the EPP. Based on these results, the EPP designs targeted workshop
                         sessions to equip candidates with the necessary competencies. This systemati                         c process
                         ensures students are thoroughly prepared for licensure assessments and their future roles as
                         educators. Furthermore, the EPP consulted with the MOE to receive updates on the licensing

              2.3 Satisfaction of Program Completers

                        The effectiveness of the EPP's programs in preparing graduates to become skilled teachers is
                   reviewed each year. This evaluation, which is closely tied to their employment outcomes, collects
                   valuable feedback  across several key  areas  of the teaching  framework:  lesson  planning  and
                   preparation,  classroom  management,  instructional  delivery,  and  professional  responsibilities.
                   These areas are assessed based on the completers’ satisfaction with their program preparation
                   and relevance in their current teaching practice. The EPP's Alumni Affairs Unit conducts the survey,
                   and the Quality Assurance Laboratory (QAL) analyzes the results. Findings from this process guide
                   ongoing  improvements  to  the  EPP's  programs,  ensuring  future                   completers      are  even  better
                   prepared for their teaching careers.

                   Early Childhood Education (ECE), five from Special Education (SPED), and one from Health and

                   positive response to their program preparation. Completers expressed being highly prepared in
                   instruction,    while professional responsibilities received the lowest              —but still highly favorable       —
                   rating across the domains assessed.
