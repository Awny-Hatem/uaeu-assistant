## GELW111 Advanced Arabic Language Skills for Law
This course has two main objectives: First, to make law students aware of the importance of language, helping them understand that mastering it allows for precise legal expression and provides a correct understanding of legal texts. Second, to develop their speaking and linguistic abilities, enhancing their capacity to analyze and communicate legal discourse effectively. Therefore, the course is divided into four units: Grammar, Analytical Reading, Legal Writing, and Speaking. The first unit (Grammar) aims to ensure students master key grammatical concepts based on their high school studies, with an emphasis on essential grammar rules. The second unit (Analytical Reading) is designed to teach students how to read legal texts accurately and analyze them linguistically and semantically, facilitating correct understanding and interpretation. The third unit (Legal Writing) focuses on training students to write legally precise texts, free of grammatical and stylistic errors, and to practice writing on topics relevant to their studies in law school. The fourth unit (Speaking) aims to equip students with the skills for effective legal delivery and the correct oral presentation of legal texts.
Advanced Arabic Language Skills for Law (GELW111) > Credit Hours : 3

Advanced Arabic Language Skills for Law (GELW111) > Course Learning Outcomes
At the end of the course, students will be able to :

Explain the content of readable legal texts.

Use classical Arabic in functional Writing.

Deliver speeches to audience with confidence and proper language.

## PUBL329 Principles of Jurisprudence
Official course record: https://www.uaeu.ac.ae/en/catalog/courses/course_2964.shtml?id=PUBL329
In this course, the student becomes familiar with the concept of the science of Usul al-Fiqh, its subject matter, benefits, sources, origins, and schools. The course also introduces the theory of the Islamic legal ruling, including the lawgiver, the ruling in its two categories—obligatory (taklifi) and declaratory (wadi‘)—as well as the subject of the ruling and the person to whom it applies. Furthermore, the student explores the discussions on semantic indications according to the jurists, including: types of clear expressions—zahir (apparent), nass (explicit), mufassar (elucidated), and muhkam (unequivocal); types of ambiguous expressions—khafi (latent), mushkil (problematic), mujmal (general), and mutashabih (equivocal); types of expressions based on the assignment of meaning—‘amm (general) and its specification, and khas (specific) and its categories such as command and prohibition, absolute and restricted, and homonymous; types of expressions based on their usage—literal and figurative, explicit and implicit; and types of expressions based on how they signify meaning—through explicit wording, indication, implication, presumption, and the concept of opposition.
Principles of Jurisprudence (PUBL329) > Credit Hours : 3

## PUBL636 Thesis

The student is committed to conduct original research on a vital topic of private law under the supervision of his or her academic supervisor, which will be presented in the form of a thesis written according to a publishable standard. The writing stage must be preceded by holding a seminar for the student to discuss the topic of the thesis with the relevant faculty members in a public session. During the writing stage, the student and supervisor must meet regularly to discuss progress and issues related to the topic of the thesis, and the supervisor is committed to providing the scientific department with a periodic report on the stages of completion. The thesis must demonstrate the student’s knowledge and familiarity with the relevant literature and the methodology. The thesis must also be accompanied by specific results and recommendations that are applicable in practice. The form of the thesis must comply with the regulations stated in the Thesis and Dissertation Preparation Guidelines, issued by the College of Graduate Studies, and it must pass the stage of scrutiny and verification of the absence of plagiarism or scientific theft through certain programs, including Turnitin. After completing writing it, it will be discussed in a public session by a specialized committee that includes an external examiner and an internal examiner headed by the supervisor.
