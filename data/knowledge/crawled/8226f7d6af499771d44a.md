                                         Dear Students,

                                         In  your  journey  to  becoming  skilled  and  compassionate
                                         doctors, I want to highlight a few guiding principles that are
                                         essential for your success. First and foremost, consistent
                                         attendance and professionalism are fundamental. As future
                                         doctors,  your  presence  is  critical  for  patients  in  urgent
                                         conditions, allowing you to respond promptly. Any delay in
                                         your attendance could risk a patient’s life.

                                         You will hold a position of trust, depended on by patients in
                                         their times of need. To earn this trust, you must be reliable
                                         and accountable. Just as you are expected to be there for
        Prof. Fatma Al Jassmi            patients when they need you, being consistently present and
Acting Dean of College of Medicine       engaged in your training      is crucial to your growth as a
 & Health Sciences, UAE University       competent physician.


Additionally, approach your studies with a genuine passion for knowledge. True
mastery   in   medicine   comes   from   a   deep   commitment   to   learning   for
understanding, not merely achieving high marks. Embrace curiosity, ask questions,
and challenge assumptions. By cultivating a genuine dedication to learning, the
marks will naturally follow as a byproduct of your competence and preparation.
Let us strive to become the kind of doctors our patients deserve—competent,
thoughtful, and ever-curious.

Finally, your insight is invaluable to us. We welcome your feedback on how we can
improve the learning experience and better support your development. Please feel
free to share your ideas with us, as your continuous feedback is essential to
improving our program for everyone.

Thank you for your commitment, and let’s work together to make this learning
journey meaningful and impactful.

 Mentorship is a vital tradition in medicine, and CMHS encourages every student, from Years 1-6, to
 benefit from it. A mentor offers a safe space to explore ideas, discuss challenges, and work
 towards personal and academic goals. For first and second-year students, mentors are assigned to
 help them adjust to CMHS. From Year 3 onwards, students can choose their mentors based on
 their academic or clinical interests.

 To request a mentor, students can contact Ms. Asma in the Office of Student Affairs. If you know
 your preferred mentor, Ms. Asma and Dr. Susan can assist with matching you. Meetings are
 encouraged at least four times a year, with flexibility as needed.

 Additionally, senior students in the PASS program are available as mentors, offering peer support
 for those navigating similar experiences.

                                                TELL US ABOUT YOUR CAREER JOURNEY AND INTERESTS BEYOND WORK

                                          I’m a specialist psychiatrist working at Sheikh Khalifa Medical City, where I also
                                          trained and have been privileged to gain experience in assessing and treating a wide
                                          variety of psychiatric cases from multiple cultures. As the largest psychiatric facility
                                          in the country, SKMC has provided an exceptional foundation for my career.

                                          This year, I’m excited to be moving to London for specialty training at the Maudsley
                                          toward a CCT. This move is the realization of a career-long dream, as I’ll be training
                                          under experts who publish the guidelines I’ve used throughout my training and
                                          clinical practice. I can’t wait for this new chapter.

                                          Outside of work, I have a deep love for art, film, and literature - all forms of human
                                          expression. Since medical school, I’ve been making short films that explore social
                                          issues, such as substance misuse. Two of my films were screened at the Cannes Film
                                          Festival, and another was shown on BBC Arabia.

          Dr. Alaa Galadari               My husband and I also welcomed our first son this year. We’re excited to raise him
 Specialsist Psychiatrist, SKMC           with a love for science, art, and hiking, nurturing the same curiosity and passion we
        CMHS Class of 2014                share.


                   WHAT MOMENTS FROM YOUR TIME AT CMHS-UAEU DO YOU APPRECIATE THE MOST?

CMHS was where I spent some of the best years of my life. It’s where I met my now best friend and an incredible group
of women I call my second family. Many of my fondest memories from CMHS revolve around them whether it was
studying together, supporting one another, or working side by side in student leadership roles. I had the honor of serving
as president of the student council, with my friends elected to different roles. This experience brought us even closer, as
we learned to lead, face challenges, and grow together. That journey helped shape our characters in ways I’ll always
carry with me.

I feel incredibly grateful to have studied medicine at such a respected institution, where professors were deeply
committed to providing us with the best education and guidance.


                                WHAT ADVICE WOULD YOU GIVE TO CURRENT CMHS STUDENTS?

Make the most of these years, and when you graduate, choose the specialty you truly love, not the one others say is
'better.' Remember that listening to others’ advice often means inviting in a blend of their principles, values, and

pursue the career I wanted. At 3 AM, when you're with a patient, it’s your love for the work that fuels you. When you

As a psychiatrist, I find the work incredibly rewarding, though I know it’s not for everyone. I would say the ones who join
