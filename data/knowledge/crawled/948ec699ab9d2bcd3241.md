## SLP100 Linguistics for Speech Language Pathology
This course is a practical introduction to the aspects of linguistics that speech and language pathologists (SLPs) need to understand in order to be able to diagnose impaired language by using the available assessments and tools for analyzing patients’ language abilities. It introduces the concepts and terminology of traditional linguistic description alongside those employed within speech and language therapy, enabling students to explore different linguistic components in detail, focusing on the core linguistic areas of phonology, morphology, syntax, semantics, discourse and pragmatics. During the course, students will gain a better understanding of how the insights from linguistic theory and analysis can be applied to the description and analysis of communication impairments.
Linguistics for Speech Language Pathology (SLP100) > Credit Hours : 3

Linguistics for Speech Language Pathology (SLP100) > Course Learning Outcomes
At the end of the course, students will be able to :

Describe basic components of grammatical description and language use, including phonetics, phonology, morphology, syntax, semantics, pragmatics, and discourse.

Illustrate how any of the basic components of grammar can become impaired.

Compare various approaches to the analysis of typical and atypical speech and language.

Develop assessments of typical and atypical speech and language.

## SLP101 Language Development and Impairment
Official course record: https://www.uaeu.ac.ae/en/catalog/courses/course_4983.shtml?id=SLP101
The course provides a basic introduction of how language develops from infancy through adulthood, examining both typical and atypical trajectories. Students will study the cognitive, biological, and social foundations of language acquisition, as well as factors that can lead to language delays or disorders. The course will cover various language impairments, including developmental language disorder (DLD), autism spectrum disorders, aphasia, and speech sound disorders, along with various language assessment strategies.

Language Development and Impairment (SLP101) > Course Learning Outcomes
At the end of the course, students will be able to :

Define key milestones in typical language development across different age ranges.

Explain the difference between typical and atypical language development, including the role of biological, cognitive, and social factors.

Discuss diagnostic criteria to identify potential language impairments in case studies.

Contrast different types of language impairments (e.g., developmental language disorder, aphasia) and their implications for intervention and support strategies.

## SLP216 Social Aspects of Communication
Official course record: https://www.uaeu.ac.ae/en/catalog/courses/course_4983.shtml?id=SLP216
This course studies the relation between language and society, focusing on language variation and change across different communication contexts and across different types of social groups. Students will be introduced to issues related to speech communities, bilingualism and multilingualism, diglossia, pidgin and creole languages, slangs and class, age, and gender-based language varieties, language policy implementation, and conversational analysis. Theoretical discussions will be supported by empirical work using linguistic quantitative methods to engage questions about the social conditioning of linguistic variation and language change both in small-scale interactions as well as in macro-social patterns of variation. The course will additionally briefly discuss socially based aspects of communicative disorders and how a good understanding of cultural and individual diversity can enhance clinical practice and research.

Social Aspects of Communication (SLP216) > Course Learning Outcomes

## SLP290 Arabic Language Impairment

This course provides a holistic study of the Arabic language including its grammatical structure and the typical and atypical development of child Arabic, focusing on Emirati Arabic with additional data drawn from other Arabic dialects, including the Modern Standard variety.
