The Chemistry Club at UAE University is a student organization interested in dispersing
chemistry education and awareness throughout the University and community. It pro-
vides academic and social support for students studying chemistry through presenta-
tions, workshops, demonstrations, tours, and attending and hosting local and national
lecturers to discuss the latest in chemistry research.

It was established five years ago by an initiative of active students at the chemistry
department. Over the past few years, the chemistry club has initiated many activities
through which the students were involved; such as, the World Science day, Chemistry
Olympiads, Chemistry week, and Chemistry day.

“Rietaj” is an Arabic word which means the Great Door. This name was chosen as a
symbol of the role this magazine plays as it allows you to enter the world of Chemis-
try, the central Science . The goal of this magazine is to provide a platform for Chem-
istry students and faculty to communicate to others in this field, as well as the students
of United Arab Emirates University about the happenings of the Chemistry depart-
ment.

Our aim: Observe, understand, learn, spread the word.

Editor’s note
It’s been a while. One year has brought us many developments on campus and in the
department, let alone our own personal courses that I am beginning to feel the years grow
on me. Being a third year student now and the President of the Chemistry Club, it was a
vastly different, experience editing and designing this issue and I am confident you will
enjoy it much more than the previous one. We all learn from our mistakes and as a
scientist, it would be very ignorant to claim that I don’t, since science is always trying
something  new.  I  am  grateful  to  some  wonderful  contributions  from  the  chemistry
department students and faculty in this issue. We have a feature on Colloids (pg. 24) by one
of our faculty members, which is surprisingly one of the topics that fascinated me even
prior to reading the article. In addition, we have two very interesting and informative
articles in Arabic about the names of elements (pg 22) and an interesting personality ‘Vick
Tandy’(pg 40).

This issue is a little different from the previous ones as we had topics that most people
would feel curious about, like ‘Can chocolate make us happy?’ and ‘How do we love in
chemistry?’ (pg 20). Growing old is another issue that interests both genders alike and you
can read about how  anti oxidants help to stop it, from a chemical perspective of a
dietician (pg. 18)

On the left is a photograph I took during my lab work for a research project that I am
currently working with.  It was at this time that I realized how much we are missing  as
chemists when I read that organic chemistry in the early times was  advanced only
through the love for a young student’s love for pigments and his desire to synthesize one in
the lab instead of getting it from natural plant and animal sources. The new section
called ‘The Art of Chemistry’ addresses this very motivation with the hope that all those
who see it would be passionate  about what you  are learning  being a student of Chemistry
- something that you  can use to build your own unique motivations.

 Happy reacting!
